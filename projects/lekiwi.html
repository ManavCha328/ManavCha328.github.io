01

Problem

Mobile manipulators make good research platforms, but their cost keeps them out of reach for most students and small labs.

The goal was a robot cheap enough to build at home, without giving up the capability that makes it useful for research: a base that drives anywhere and an arm that can pick things up.

02

Approach

I owned the complete hardware architecture through a three-month design sprint: component selection, chassis CAD in Fusion 360, power, and actuation.

Three omni wheels give holonomic motion: the robot drives in any direction and turns independently, so it can line the arm up with an object without extra maneuvering.

03

Design iterations

V1 went from Fusion 360 to a working robot. V2 was rebuilt around Dynamixel servos, with open-source integration work alongside Hugging Face LeRobot and ROBOTIS.

04

Open-source build guide

I wrote the build guide the community builds from: a bill of materials, 3D-printing instructions, and step-by-step assembly with photos of every step, plus a separate guide for the Dynamixel version.

05

Testing

Validated in MuJoCo simulation and on hardware: kinematics, control response, and sensor and actuator integration.

06

Results

  • 1,480+ GitHub stars, 160+ forks, and 100s of community builds
  • An official platform in Hugging Face’s LeRobot ecosystem
  • Under $500 in total parts: the lowest-cost open-source mobile platform in its class
  • $40,000+ in corporate sponsorship secured
